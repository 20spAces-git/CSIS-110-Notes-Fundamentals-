# 💾 Computer Storage (SSD & HDD)

## 📖 What is Computer Storage?

Computer storage is where a computer keeps information for **long-term use**.

Storage can hold things such as:

- 📸 Pictures
- 🎵 Music
- 🎮 Games
- 📄 Documents
- 🎬 Videos
- 💻 Programs and applications
- 🪟 The operating system

Unlike RAM, information saved to storage normally remains there even
after the computer is turned off.

---

## 🖼️ What Does Computer Storage Look Like?

![Example of computer storage](storage-example.jpg)

The image above shows an example of a storage device used inside a
computer.

Storage devices can look different depending on the type of computer
and the type of storage being used.

Two types that beginners will commonly hear about are:

- ⚡ **SSD — Solid-State Drive**
- 💿 **HDD — Hard Disk Drive**

We will compare both below.

---

## 💡 A Simple Way to Think About Storage

Remember how we compared RAM to a **desk**?

Think of storage like a **filing cabinet**. 🗄️

Imagine you're working on a school assignment.

The papers you're currently working with might be sitting on your desk.

When you're finished, you put them into a filing cabinet so you can
find them again later.

Our computer comparison would be:

- 🧠 **RAM = Desk**
- 💾 **Storage = Filing Cabinet**
- ⚙️ **CPU = Person doing the work**

This isn't exactly how a computer works internally, but it's an easy
way for a beginner to understand the different jobs.

---

## 🌎 Real-World Example

Imagine you write a school paper using Microsoft Word. 📝

You click **Save** before turning off your computer.

Later, you:

1. Turn the computer back on.
2. Open Microsoft Word.
3. Find your document.
4. Continue working on it.

Your paper didn't disappear when the computer was turned off because
it was saved to long-term storage.

---

# 💿 What is an HDD?

HDD stands for **Hard Disk Drive**.

An HDD is a storage device that uses spinning magnetic disks to store
information.

HDDs contain moving mechanical parts.

### 💡 Beginner Example

Think about a record player. 🎵

A record spins while another part of the machine interacts with the
record.

An HDD is much more complicated than a record player, but the comparison
can help us understand that an HDD has **physical moving parts inside it**.

---

# ⚡ What is an SSD?

SSD stands for **Solid-State Drive**.

An SSD stores information using **flash memory**.

Unlike an HDD, an SSD does not need spinning disks to store and access
information.

SSDs are commonly found in modern computers.

---

## 🌎 Real-World SSD Example

Imagine two similar computers.

One uses an older HDD and the other uses an SSD.

The computer using the SSD may:

- 🚀 Start Windows faster
- 📂 Open files faster
- 💻 Load programs faster
- 🎮 Load some games faster

This is one reason SSDs have become common in modern computers.

---

## 🆚 SSD vs. HDD

| ⚡ SSD | 💿 HDD |
|---|---|
| Uses flash memory | Uses magnetic disks |
| No spinning disks | Contains spinning disks |
| Generally faster | Generally slower |
| Usually costs more per amount of storage | Often cheaper per amount of storage |
| Common in modern computers | Still useful for large amounts of storage |

Neither one is automatically "bad."

The right storage device depends on what someone needs the computer to do.

---

# 🔢 What Are GB and TB?

When looking at storage, you may see numbers such as:

- 256 GB
- 512 GB
- 1 TB
- 2 TB

### 📖 GB

**GB** stands for **Gigabyte**.

### 📖 TB

**TB** stands for **Terabyte**.

These are units used to describe how much information a storage device
can hold.

A terabyte is larger than a gigabyte.

For example:

**1 TB = approximately 1,000 GB** when storage manufacturers describe
drive capacity.

---

## ⚠️ Capacity Does NOT Mean Speed

This is an important beginner concept.

Imagine these two drives:

**Drive A:** 1 TB HDD

**Drive B:** 512 GB SSD

Drive A has more storage capacity.

That does NOT automatically mean Drive A is faster.

💡 **Capacity tells us how much information can be stored.**

⚡ **Speed tells us how quickly information can be accessed or transferred.**

They describe different things.

---

# 🧠 RAM vs. Storage

RAM and storage can both be measured using GB, which can make them
confusing for beginners.

But they have different jobs.

| 🧠 RAM | 💾 Storage |
|---|---|
| Short-term memory | Long-term storage |
| Used while programs are running | Stores programs and files |
| Normally loses its information when power is removed | Keeps saved information after shutdown |
| Helps with actively used information | Keeps information for future use |

A computer might have:

**16 GB RAM**

and

**512 GB SSD storage**

Those numbers are describing two completely different parts of the
computer.

---

# 🛠️ Why Does Storage Matter in IT?

Understanding storage is useful when troubleshooting computer problems.

**Troubleshooting** means identifying what may be causing a problem and
trying to find a solution.

A user might tell an IT support technician:

> "My computer says I'm running out of storage."

The technician may need to check:

- 📊 How much total storage the computer has
- 🔴 How much is being used
- 🟢 How much is available
- 📁 What files or programs are using the space

Storage knowledge is also useful when upgrading computers, installing
programs, replacing drives, and helping users manage their files.

## 🖥️ Step 1: Open Settings

### Method 1 — Keyboard Shortcut

Press:

**Windows Key + I**

This should open Windows **Settings**.

### Method 2 — Windows Search

You can also:

1. Click the **Start** button.

Once Settings opens:

1. Click **System**.
2. Scroll until you find **Storage**.
3. Click **Storage**.

Windows should now display information about how your storage is being
used.

Your screen may look slightly different depending on your version of
Windows.

---

## 🔍 Step 3: Look Around

You may see categories such as:

- 💻 Installed apps
- 📄 Documents
- 🖼️ Pictures
- 🗑️ Temporary files
- ⚙️ System files
- 📁 Other files

Don't worry if you don't understand every category yet.

For now, we're simply learning how to find this information.

# 🔗 Putting Everything Together

We've now learned about three important parts of a computer.

### ⚙️ CPU
Processes instructions.

### 🧠 RAM
Temporarily holds information that is actively being used.

### 💾 Storage
Keeps files, programs, and other information for long-term use.

They have different jobs, but they work together to help a computer
operate.
